References to `flower.js` describe the renderer at that time; the current implementation lives in `src/garden/engine.ts`, `src/garden/petal-meshes.js`, and `src/garden/extra-blooms.js`. Some `/tmp/` captures cited below were temporary and are not repository assets. Use `DESIGN.md` for the current interface system and the committed screenshots in `docs/images/` for its present appearance. The findings below are not an unresolved implementation checklist or a review of the current release.

## First bouquet pass: fix the rose before further styling

`/tmp/bouquet-first.png` shows the first working bouquet: it looks hand-tied, the colors are more plausible, and Surprise Me replaces the selectors. Its most visible defect is the dark, torn-looking facets **inside all three rose heads** (amber at the top and both ivory heads). It is not a collision between neighboring flowers. Seed **`1795603375315`** reproduces “A Little Wild” with seven stems, a cosmos/rose/lily mix, and 9×6, 8×6, and 8×5 rose counts (count × layers before reducing inner whorl counts).

- **Why the petals cut through each other:** `buildBloom` still gives each rose a generic radial ribbon, up to `.64` half-width, and anchors all whorls within `.025–.048` of the bloom axis. For the screenshot's outer rose layers at `t=.5`, approximate edge radii from the renderer's transforms are `.61–.65`, and a nonoverlapping sector would limit half-width to `.225–.270` (`r*tan(π/count)`). Actual half-widths are `.615–.675`: **2.3–3.0×** that bound. The symmetric raised edges cross adjacent raised edges instead of lying one over the next; additional angled tiers give more chances to cut across them. Exact attribution of individual triangles to crossing vs. bump/normal artifacts needs a rerender. It's not safe to call the whole artifact z-fighting: the strips need not be coplanar to cut through visibly.
- **First diagnostic:** hold the screenshot seed, disable bump and vertex patterns, set rose ruffle to zero, and render flat-colored petals with smooth normals. Temporarily give each whorl a different flat color and toggle inner whorls off, then back on one at a time. Inspect mature front and 30° views. This isolates same-whorl versus tier collisions; lowering bump alone will not solve the packing.
- **Fastest robust fix:** use three nested rose cups, each made from angular-sector panels. At longitudinal knots `[0,.22,.50,.78,1]`, set radial/axial profiles `(r/R, y/R)` as follows: outer (8 petals): `r=[.18,.34,.62,.84,1]`, `y=[0,.04,.12,.18,.14]`; middle (6): `r=[.09,.17,.30,.48,.64]`, `y=[.17,.22,.31,.39,.36]`; inner (5): `r=[.035,.06,.11,.20,.30]`, `y=[.37,.42,.51,.60,.57]`. Use non-overshooting interpolation. Per ring, `pitch=2π/N`, `theta=theta_i + twist*t + (pitch * halfSpan(t))*s` (`s∈[-1,1]`, `halfSpan=[.12,.38,.59,.59,.51]`, `twist~.14` rad). Set `y/R=profileY(t)+.028*s+.006*s²`; the signed rise orders overlaps within a common surface of revolution, unlike the old symmetric raised edges. Round the petal lip by limiting its sampled `t` to `1-.10*s²`. Offset the middle ring phase about half a pitch. Retain at least `.06R` of layer-to-layer separation **at the same radius** (including after jitter); close the inner hole with 2–3 nested lips, never a gold disk. Don't reuse the old independent pivot angles on the mature cups; animate the head and use a shared cup morph per whorl if needed. This is a proposed starting mesh, not a rendered result.
- **If the old builder must ship temporarily:** use 3 whorls at 8/6/5 petals and cap half-width in the mature geometry to `.95*r*tan(π/N)` (radius **after** pitch) so the panels have nonoverlapping footprints. Keep .06R of separation between whorls and remove the `i*.003` ramp. This sacrifices some overlap and fullness; widen the rim rather than restoring mid-petal width, and prefer the polar shingle when possible. Rose test material: roughness `.74–.82`, clearcoat `0–.04`, sheen `.1–.2`, bump off or `.006` after it reads clean.
- **Next geometry pass:** lily in the bouquet already reads better than the single-stem sample; keep its orange/spots and make six anthers plus one long style legible, narrow the outer three tepals, and remove the septet calyx star and gold puck. The wide flat cosmos needs two tiny apical notches and threadlike foliage to look like cosmos rather than a generic blossom. Use the species-specific widths and centers below. Keep the pigment constraints already shipped, but tie cultivar choices within repeated species so one bouquet has a coherent 2–3 color scheme. Optional composition improvement after the rose: give one focal head a 12–18% size advantage and widen the flanks ~10%, rather than zooming the whole bouquet and clipping the cut ends.

The rest of this document records the baseline review and more detailed geometry options. The early priorities below reflect the pre-bouquet source; the rose diagnosis above takes precedence for the current renderer.

## What to change first

1. **Restrict the colors by species.** The quickest identity gains are white daisy rays, gold sunflower rays against a dark disk, and scarlet poppy petals with a dark throat. Keep surprise in cultivar, proportion and arrangement, not an unrestricted hue on every flower.
2. **Replace the shared pointed ribbon profile.** Keep the mesh infrastructure, but drive it with species-specific width, centerline and rim curves. Build a separate cup for the tulip and spiral crowns for the rose and ball dahlia. Turn off ornamental patterns until the silhouettes read at bouquet size.
3. **Use species-specific centers and conceal the attachment hardware.** Disk florets belong to daisy/sunflower/cosmos, not poppy/tulip/lily. Lily needs one style as well as six stamens. Remove the seven-sepal star from tulip and lily altogether.
4. **Compose a handful of stems, not a grid of little flowers.** Keep a large focal bloom and four supporting heads, and make their cut ends converge. At mobile size preserve faces and centers before adding more stems.

## Findings from the existing implementation

- `flower.js`, `botanicalGeometry`: the same `sin(πt)^shape` width profile narrows both ends for every petal. At the base the entire first row is coincident; the grid emits 18 zero-area triangles there. At the tip the width is nearly zero but the edge wave is still active, so the points do not converge cleanly. The large edge cup, old bump strength and interleaving surfaces are plausible contributors to the papery facets visible in the dahlia and rose. The exact cause of each artifact is not established from stills.
- `flower.js`, `buildFlower`: all heads repeat equally spaced radials with a common attachment radius per layer. The rose uses the golden angle **between whole rings**, not between petals; the dahlia uses staggered rings. The disk sampling has a good golden-angle start. The `i * .003` height bias introduces a one-sided ramp with a step at the wraparound; use bounded per-petal jitter instead. The lily already has a nominal 60° offset between its 3+3 whorls; the screenshot nevertheless reads as three twin blades, so changing the angle alone is not the fix.
- `flower.js`, center and foliage: every visible center begins as the same flattened sphere plus 260 or 800 bead instances. Poppy filaments sit around this fake disk; lily and tulip get theirs over it. All flowers use the same star of seven sepals, and foliage differences are largely ribbon widths and serration. The tulip's exposed green star is especially distracting because its bright purple cup otherwise reads fairly well.
- `botany.js`, `createSpecimen`: the full hue wheel and shared bicolor/speckle lottery produce purple daisy, lime-green cosmos and rose, teal dahlia, and pale green poppy in the example captures. The sunflower's disk and proportions are already recognizable, but the hard red/cream division and oval leaves pull it away from a classic sunflower. The poppy has the right broad, crumpled mass; use that as a starting point.
- `flower.js`, `botanicalGeometry`: the mesh reads a mutable outer `specimen` for recipe and pattern. Pass the specimen or its explicit geometry profile to each builder so two bouquet stems cannot inherit each other's settings. Keep the same seed through replay and save; derive independent layout and per-stem random streams so editing the tessellation cannot reshuffle the bouquet.

## Mesh revision: a shared representation, not a shared outline

The numbers below are starting points for rendering, not measurements from plants. Use **R = mature outer head radius** (excluding stem, measured perpendicular to the bloom axis). Heights and widths below are multiples of R. For an open unit ray in its local frame, sample longitudinal `t ∈ [0,1]`, lateral `s ∈ [-1,1]`, with `+z` outward and `+y` towards the opening. `z/R = rootRadius + L*t - capInset(t)*s²`; `x/R = s*w(t)` where `w` is **half-width**; `y/R = h(t) + edgeLift*s²*widthEnvelope(t) + ripple`. The width arrays below are at `t = [0,.20,.50,.80,1]`. Interpolate without overshooting. For rounded rims use `capInset=.04–.08*smoothstep(.70,1,t)`; zero the inset for a true pointed cap.

For true point ends, emit one vertex with a triangle fan and fade the ripple and edge lift to zero there. For broad distal rims keep a finite end row and curve it back with the inset; don't collapse it. Use `|s|³` to keep any edge ripple out of the central lamina. Mild changes per petal are enough: angle ±1.5°, length ±4%, width ±5%, elevation ±.007R; use intentional whorl offsets for overlaps. Start at 18–24 longitudinal × 8–12 transverse segments (less for crowded small florets), roughness `.65–.8`, clearcoat `0–.06`, and shared bump scale `.006–.012` at the current approximately unit-sized geometry. Turn the bump map off during silhouette tuning; compare it back on after faceting is fixed.

### The three that merit dedicated builders

- **Tulip: six overlapping tepal shells, not a radial flower rotated upwards.** Use cylindrical coordinates around the open axis, with outer angles `0/120/240°` and inner angles `60/180/300°`. At `t=[0,.22,.52,.80,1]` use `r/R=[.07,.54,.95,1,.79]`, `y/R=[0,.28,.72,1.24,1.60]`, angular half-span `[.10,.49,.65,.65,.49]` radians; `θ=θ₀+s*halfSpan`, and depress the rounded side corners by `.11R*s²*smoothstep(.72,1,t)`. Inner tepal radius × `.93`, height × `1.03`, angular half-span × `.95`: overlap through the shoulder, but keep a subtly scalloped aperture. Render near upright (axis ~15–25° toward the camera from world up), so both the cup and a little opening show. Put six short dark stamens and one pale pistil low inside; no green calyx star. Two long waxy, folded blades should clasp the lower stem and curve upwards.
- **Rose: distinct broad guard petals and a high, winding center.** Use 7–9 guards, then 6–8 transition petals shifted about half a petal spacing, then 15–19 individually staggered petals. For the guards: `r=.20`, `L=.80`, widths `[.035,.17,.32,.35,.23]`, centerline heights `[0,.04,.14,.19,.07]`, edge lift `.085`, cap inset `.07`. Transition petals: `r=.13`, `L=.60`, widths × `.80`, heights `[.09,.17,.31,.40,.36]`. For the remaining petals indexed outer to inner, `u=i/(n-1)`, `θ=i*2.39996323+phase`, radius at base `.12→.025`, radius at tip `.59→.10`, height at base `.14→.37`, height at tip `.42→.68`, maximum half-width `.22→.065`; ease between these values and keep broad rounded ends. Add `.12–.22` radians of twist and a gentle outward roll of the lip; use 2–3 curved closing petals if the center gaps. No disk. Add 5 slim reflexed sepals tucked underneath and a 3/5-leaflet compound leaf, not a lone toothed blade.
- **Dahlia: pick a ball dahlia, densely packed rolled florets.** Sample ~96 individual sockets / tip directions with `u=sqrt((i+.5)/96)`, `θ=i*2.39996323+phase`, starting center-out. Set tip `r/R=.035+.965u`, `y/R=.12+.70sqrt(1-u²)`, base radius `r*(.40+.18u)` and base height `y-(.24-.08u)R`. Connect with smooth, outward-facing centerlines. Cross section should be an incurved arc, not an `s²` sheet: local lateral offset `a*sin(β*s)`, normal offset `a*(1-cos(β*s))`, with `β=90–110°`, and peak `a/R=.047+.043u` rescaled by a width envelope that is narrow at the base and rounded at the end. Rotate each floret's cross-section with the centerline frame. Tune packing in side view; keep the midpoint spacing and overlaps close, with no isolated spiky tips or exposed flat yellow button. Close any visible center hole with 3–5 tiny tightly rolled florets. Near-opposite foliage can use broad serrated leaflets.

### Disk florets and color

- Keep golden-angle sampling for the three composite disks. `r_i=.96*r_disk*sqrt((i+.5)/N)`; use a shallow dome and orient instances to the local normal. Scale floret radius around `.70–.82*r_disk/sqrt(N)` instead of a fixed fraction of disk radius at all densities. Low-poly tubes or trimmed cones with darker/occluded crowns give more structure than glossy spheres; golden open florets near a sunflower disk's border are distinct from the dark, tight interior buds.
- Suggested base swatches (sRGB), then seed small variation within the chosen cultivar. Vary saturation/lightness and basal-to-tip values more than hue (usually `±5°` hue, `±5` lightness points). For white petals, vary cream versus warm white but leave enough contrast against the background. Save strong bicolor for a curated pattern with a biological placement, e.g. basal poppy mask or lily throat/spots.

## Surprise bouquet layout

- Start with **5 stems** from a seeded focal/support pool. One hero head (sunflower, rose, dahlia, lily or tulip) plus 4 supports across 2–3 additional species; repeat one supporting species at another scale and orientation. Let the bouquet draw from warm, blush/cream or high-contrast palette families and weight the species/cultivar combinations, rather than uniformly mixing arbitrary hues. Prefer surprise composition over user-facing species or color selectors.
- Example mature head centers `(x,y,z)` in scene units with positive z toward the camera. Hero `(0,2.18,.40), R=.98`; rear left `(-1.08,3.04,-.24), R=.56`; rear right `(1.19,3.11,-.31), R=.60`; low left `(-1.36,1.86,.17), R=.54`; low right `(1.39,1.77,.12), R=.58`. With 6 stems, add a small crest near `(.12,3.54,-.55), R=.35`. Jitter up to `.12` on x/y and `.10` on z, then test projection: allow petals to overlap around 10–18% of the smaller face width, but don't hide the center of a supporting blossom. For a tulip, score the projected cup bounds instead of a face disk; keep the tulip slightly to the side of taller stems.
- Anchor all cut ends near `(0,-2.25,0)` within ellipse radii `(.26,.18)` in x/z. Use per-stem curves through a loose waist at `y=-1.4`, cross once or twice in projection, then fan toward individual heads. Keep foliage asymmetrical, mainly at `y=-.9…1.0`, extending a little beyond the envelope; do not copy 3 leaves at identical heights on 5 stems. The existing upright version simply translated into multiple x slots will look like garden rows.
- Face the focal bloom roughly toward the camera with 5–20° deviation; turn supporting faces outward another 10–25° and vary pitch. Keep the tulip cup near upright. Rotate/sway the parent bouquet so converged stems retain their relationship. Compose via projected mature head/stem/leaf bounds (incl. rotation + sway margin), frame with ~8–12% padding on the shorter dimension, and check portrait as well as desktop. Keep initial open faces separated enough that a thumbnail shows multiple silhouettes and centers.
- If rendering cost rises, instance disk florets, share textures, simplify geometry on supporting heads, and avoid duplicating fallback disk meshes. Test saved PNG and yielded pose against the on-screen mature bouquet, not just growth animation frames.
